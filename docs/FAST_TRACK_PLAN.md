# Fast-Track Revenue Plan

## Priority rule
The portfolio is now ranked by earliest credible path to first cash, not theoretical maximum valuation. Jarvis is excluded because it is an orchestration/dashboard project, not a revenue generator.

## Portfolio priority
1. Boyd's Bar — existing operating business; fastest path to cash from existing demand/assets.
2. Productized AI Service — can generate cash quickly but requires client interaction, so it is not the passive priority.
3. Service Arbitrage — potentially fast cash, but client/vendor coordination creates ongoing involvement.
4. Digital Asset Licensing — target first sale in 7–30 days with no paid acquisition and minimal owner involvement; selected as the new passive-income priority.
5. Wholesale Deal Engine — potentially large transaction revenue but deal cycles, legal/closing dependencies and human exceptions make timing less predictable.
6. Wealth Management Protection Protocol — recurring revenue potential but substantially longer build/sales cycle.
7. EventMatch — marketplace liquidity/network effects make first meaningful revenue slower.
8. Autonomous Trading System — deliberately lower priority until validation; capital and market risk make it unsuitable for the immediate cash-flow objective.
9. Jarvis — excluded from revenue ranking by instruction.

## Digital Asset Licensing execution phases

### Phase 0 — Revenue architecture (Day 0–1)
- Define product/license schema.
- Define SKU, versioning and rights ledger.
- Establish demand-score model.
- Establish quality gate and originality/provenance checks.
- Establish revenue and exception telemetry.

### Phase 1 — First-cash MVP (Days 1–7)
- Identify 3–5 narrow commercial niches with observable buyer intent.
- Produce 20–30 high-utility micro-assets.
- Favor calculators, operational tools, checklists, and business-use systems over generic content.
- Generate listings, previews, metadata, license terms and delivery packages.
- Publish through viable zero-upfront channels.
- Optimize for the earliest legitimate sale, not catalog size.

### Phase 2 — Validation loop (Days 8–30)
- Measure impressions, clicks, saves, conversion, revenue, refunds and support burden.
- Remove or revise weak SKUs.
- Expand products showing actual purchase intent.
- Build bundles only after individual SKUs prove demand.
- Target first $100 cumulative revenue.

### Phase 3 — Autopilot expansion (Days 31–90)
- Automate winner cloning into adjacent use cases without copying competitors.
- Add license tiers where justified.
- Expand distribution.
- Automate reporting and exception alerts.
- Target $500–$1,000 monthly run rate.

### Phase 4 — Compounding catalog (Months 4–9)
- Concentrate production on proven categories.
- Maintain a large long-tail catalog.
- Introduce premium commercial bundles.
- Continuously prune low performers.
- Target $1,000–$2,500+ monthly run rate.

## Cash-first rules
- No speculative six-month build before publishing.
- No paid ads during validation.
- No custom requests.
- No recurring manual fulfillment.
- No unnecessary software spend.
- Every build decision must improve time-to-first-sale, conversion, average order value, distribution, or automation.
- If a product/category cannot show demand, stop producing it.

## Owner involvement target
Initial setup and unavoidable approvals may require concentrated work. After launch, the system should operate with exception-based involvement rather than routine customer service or fulfillment.
